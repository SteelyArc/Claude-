## 1. Historical outline

### Cold open — 1936 (flash-forward)
- Archive audio: Hitler addressing workers at the Krupp works, Essen, March 1936. **Wording and date still need checking against your audio file and its source.**
- Narration puts it in context: by 1936 Germany is a dictatorship and the "election" offers a single list. *How did it get here?*

### Ch. 1 — 1918–1919: Defeat and Revolution
- November 1918: mutiny in Kiel, revolution, the Kaiser abdicates (9 Nov); Scheidemann proclaims the republic; armistice on 11 Nov.
- January 1919: the Spartacist uprising is crushed. On 19 Jan, elections to the National Assembly, the first in which women can vote.
- 28 June 1919: Treaty of Versailles. Territory lost, reparations, Article 231 (war guilt).
- 11 Aug 1919: the Weimar Constitution, including Article 48 (presidential emergency powers).
- The "stab-in-the-back" myth: a lie that blamed defeat on democrats and Jews.
- **Map:** borders of 1914 → borders of 1920 (lost territories marked, East Prussia cut off, Saar under League of Nations rule).

### Ch. 2 — 1919–1923: Hitler and the Early Nazi Movement
- September 1919: Hitler, working as an army informant, attends a meeting of the German Workers' Party (DAP) and joins it.
- 24 Feb 1920: the 25-point programme at the Hofbräuhaus in Munich; the party is renamed NSDAP. Radical nationalism and antisemitism are at its core from the start.
- July 1921: Hitler becomes party leader. The SA becomes the party's street-fighting force.
- 1923: the French occupy the Ruhr; hyperinflation (1 US dollar = 4.2 trillion marks, 20 Nov 1923).
- 8–9 Nov 1923: the **Beer Hall Putsch** in Munich (Bürgerbräukeller → Feldherrnhalle). It collapses under police fire: 4 police officers and 16 putschists are killed.
- **Map:** zoom to Munich.

### Ch. 3 — 1924–1929: A New Strategy
- 1924 trial: lenient judges let Hitler use it as a stage. Sentenced to 5 years, he serves about 9 months in Landsberg and writes *Mein Kampf*.
- 27 Feb 1925: the party is refounded. The new strategy: win power legally through elections, while despising democracy and continuing street violence.
- The "Golden Twenties": currency stabilised, the Dawes Plan, Stresemann, Locarno (1925), entry into the League of Nations (1926). Hindenburg is elected president in 1925.
- May 1928: the NSDAP wins only **2.6 %**. Its rise was not inevitable.

### Ch. 4 — 1929–1932: Depression and Breakthrough
- October 1929: the Wall Street crash. US loans are called in and German unemployment explodes (about 6 million registered in early 1932).
- March 1930: the grand coalition collapses. Brüning governs by presidential decree (Article 48), so parliament is sidelined.
- **14 Sep 1930: NSDAP 18.3 %** (107 seats).
- Propaganda machine (Goebbels), mass rallies, Hitler's campaign flights in 1932, street battles between the SA and communists (e.g. "Altona Bloody Sunday", 17 Jul 1932).
- 1932 presidential election: Hindenburg is re-elected; Hitler wins 36.8 % in the run-off.
- Papen's "cabinet of barons"; the "Prussian coup" (20 Jul 1932).
- **31 Jul 1932: NSDAP 37.3 %** (230 seats), the largest party. On 13 Aug, Hindenburg refuses to make Hitler chancellor.
- **6 Nov 1932: NSDAP 33.1 %** (196 seats). It loses 2 million votes.
- **Key fact:** the NSDAP never won an absolute majority in a free national election.
- **Map:** campaign flight routes; regional results *only if reliable constituency data can be sourced* (otherwise national figures only).

### Ch. 5 — January 1933: The Backroom Deal
- Dec 1932: Schleicher becomes chancellor and fails to split the NSDAP.
- 4 Jan 1933: Papen and Hitler meet secretly at the house of banker Kurt von Schröder in Cologne.
- Hugenberg (DNVP), Papen and Hindenburg's circle agree to a cabinet in which conservatives are meant to "box Hitler in". Only 3 Nazis sit in the cabinet: Hitler, Frick and Göring.
- **30 Jan 1933: Hindenburg appoints Hitler Reich Chancellor.** This was an appointment, not an election victory. That evening the SA holds a torchlight parade through the Brandenburg Gate.

### Ch. 6 — February–March 1933: Democracy Destroyed
- 1 Feb: the Reichstag is dissolved. 4 Feb: a decree restricts the press and public meetings. 22 Feb: the SA and SS become auxiliary police in Prussia.
- **27 Feb: the Reichstag fire.** Marinus van der Lubbe is arrested at the scene. The regime blames a communist plot. *(Who was behind the fire is still debated by historians; we label this as interpretation.)*
- **28 Feb: Reichstag Fire Decree.** Basic rights are suspended "until further notice", and thousands of communists and social democrats are arrested.
- 5 Mar election, held under terror: the NSDAP wins 43.9 %, still no majority on its own. With the DNVP it reaches a bare majority (51.9 %).
- 21 Mar: the "Day of Potsdam" (a staged handshake with Hindenburg). 22 Mar: Dachau concentration camp opens.
- **23 Mar: Enabling Act, 444 votes to 94.** The KPD deputies were already arrested or in hiding; SA and SS men lined the hall; the Centre Party voted yes. SPD leader Otto Wels spoke against it: *"Freedom and life can be taken from us, but not our honour."*

### Ch. 7 — 1933–1934: The Dictatorship Consolidated
- 1 Apr: boycott of Jewish businesses. 7 Apr: Jews are pushed out of the civil service. 10 May: book burnings.
- 2 May: trade unions smashed. June–July: the SPD is banned and all other parties dissolve. 14 Jul: the law against founding new parties.
- Early concentration camps, the Gestapo, persecution of political opponents, Jews and other targeted groups.
- 30 Jun – 2 Jul 1934: the "Night of the Long Knives". Röhm, Schleicher and others are murdered.
- **2 Aug 1934: Hindenburg dies.** Hitler merges the offices of president and chancellor, and the army swears a personal oath to him. 549 days after his appointment.

### Epilogue
- Democracy was dismantled step by step, through elections, propaganda, violence, emergency laws and elite miscalculation.
- One line looking ahead: the regime went on to wage war and carry out the Holocaust, the murder of six million Jews.
