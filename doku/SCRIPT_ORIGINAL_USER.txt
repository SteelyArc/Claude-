[deep, serious, cinematic]
Germany, 1933.
[short pause]
A nation in crisis.
[slow, grave]
A democracy under pressure.
[dramatic, lower tone]
And one man… on the verge of absolute power.
[short pause]
This is the story of how Adolf Hitler came to power.
[serious, measured]
Not through one election.
Not through one sudden revolution.
But through years of political instability, economic collapse, propaganda, violence… and decisions that would change the course of history.
[dark, slow]
The men who helped Hitler become Chancellor believed they could control him.
[short pause]
They were wrong.
[dramatic]
Within months, Germany's democracy would be dismantled.
And the world would move closer to catastrophe.
---
[serious, steady]
To understand Hitler's rise, we must return to the end of the First World War.
In 1918, Germany was defeated.
Millions of soldiers had been killed or wounded. The economy was exhausted, and the German monarchy collapsed.
In November 1918, Kaiser Wilhelm the Second abdicated.
A new democratic republic was established — later known as the Weimar Republic.
[grave]
For many Germans, the new government was associated with defeat, humiliation, and national weakness.
In 1919, Germany signed the Treaty of Versailles.
The treaty imposed territorial losses, military restrictions, and reparations.
Germany lost important territories. Its army was limited, and its political leaders faced enormous pressure.
[dark, measured]
Nationalist groups used the treaty to spread anger against the republic.
Some claimed that Germany had not truly been defeated on the battlefield… but had been betrayed by politicians at home.
This false narrative became known as the “stab-in-the-back” myth.
It was one of the lies used to undermine German democracy.
---
[serious, documentary]
Adolf Hitler was born in Austria in 1889.
After moving to Munich, he served in the German army during the First World War.
In 1919, he became involved with a small political group called the German Workers' Party.
The party promoted extreme nationalism, antisemitism, and hostility toward democracy.
Hitler quickly became one of its most effective speakers.
[slightly intense]
The party was renamed the National Socialist German Workers' Party — the Nazi Party.
Hitler understood the power of emotion.
He used speeches, dramatic gestures, and simple slogans to turn frustration into political anger.
He blamed Germany's problems on Jews, Marxists, and democratic politicians.
[grave]
These accusations were false and deeply antisemitic.
But they became central to Nazi propaganda.
---
[dark, measured]
In November 1923, Hitler attempted to overthrow the Bavarian government during the Beer Hall Putsch.
The coup failed.
Hitler was arrested and sentenced to prison.
During his imprisonment, he wrote much of his political manifesto, Mein Kampf.
[short pause]
The failed coup taught Hitler an important lesson.
He would no longer rely only on a direct seizure of power.
Instead, he would attempt to enter government through elections…
While continuing to attack the democratic system itself.
After his release, Hitler rebuilt the Nazi Party.
---
[slow, serious]
During the mid-1920s, Germany experienced a period of relative economic recovery.
The Nazi Party remained a fringe movement.
But everything changed in 1929.
[dramatic, lower tone]
The Wall Street crash triggered a global economic crisis.
German businesses collapsed.
Banks failed.
Millions of people lost their jobs.
[grave]
By the early 1930s, unemployment had reached devastating levels.
The democratic government struggled to respond.
Coalition governments broke down.
Political parties became increasingly divided.
President Paul von Hindenburg relied more and more on emergency powers.
[dark, measured]
The Nazis presented themselves as a radical alternative.
They promised jobs, national revival, strong leadership, and an end to political chaos.
Their promises were accompanied by intimidation and violence.
The Nazi paramilitary organization, the SA, fought political opponents in the streets.
Nazi propaganda reached millions through posters, newspapers, radio, and mass rallies.
[slightly intense]
The party offered different messages to different groups — appealing to workers, farmers, business owners, nationalists, and the unemployed.
Its central promise was simple.
[dramatic pause]
Germany would rise again under Hitler.
---
[serious, steady]
The Nazi breakthrough came in the elections of 1930 and 1932.
In September 1930, the Nazi Party won about eighteen percent of the vote.
In July 1932, it became the largest party in the Reichstag, receiving more than thirty-seven percent.
[measured]
But the Nazis had not won an outright majority.
In November 1932, their support fell to about thirty-three percent.
Hitler was still not Chancellor.
[short pause]
The decisive events now took place behind closed doors.
---
[dark, measured]
Germany's conservative political elites believed they could use Hitler's popularity for their own purposes.
Former Chancellor Franz von Papen played a crucial role in negotiations.
He and other conservatives believed that Hitler could be surrounded by traditional politicians…
And controlled from within the government.
[grave]
President Hindenburg had previously refused to appoint Hitler as Chancellor.
But political deadlock continued.
[dramatic, slow]
On the thirtieth of January, 1933…
Hindenburg appointed Adolf Hitler as Chancellor of Germany.
[short pause]
Hitler had not won a majority in a free national election.
He had been appointed through a political deal.
[dark, deliberate]
The men who helped bring him into office believed they had found a way to use him.
They had badly underestimated him.
---
[tense, serious]
Once in power, Hitler moved quickly.
In February 1933, the Reichstag building in Berlin caught fire.
The Nazis blamed communists and used the event to intensify their campaign against political opponents.
[dark, measured]
Hindenburg signed the Reichstag Fire Decree.
Civil liberties were suspended.
Police gained sweeping powers.
Political opponents were arrested.
Newspapers were suppressed.
Many people were imprisoned without normal legal protections.
[short pause]
The atmosphere of fear helped the Nazis prepare for the next election.
In March 1933, the Nazi Party increased its vote…
But still failed to win an absolute majority on its own.
The government now sought a law that would allow Hitler to rule without parliamentary approval.
---
[grave, deliberate]
On the twenty-third of March, 1933, the Reichstag debated the Enabling Act.
The law would allow Hitler's government to pass legislation without the Reichstag…
Even legislation that violated the constitution.
[dark]
Communist deputies had been arrested or excluded.
Many Social Democratic deputies faced intimidation.
Despite the threats, the Social Democrats voted against the act.
Most other parties supported it.
[dramatic, slow]
The Enabling Act passed.
[short pause]
It gave Hitler's government a legal mechanism to dismantle the democratic system.
But the process was not truly democratic.
It took place under conditions of terror, intimidation, and the suppression of opposition.
---
[serious, steady]
Over the following months, the Nazis destroyed Germany's political freedoms.
Trade unions were dissolved.
Other political parties were banned or forced to disband.
The press was brought under Nazi control.
Political opponents were arrested.
[dark, grave]
The first concentration camps were established to imprison those considered enemies of the regime.
Jewish people faced increasing discrimination and persecution.
The Nazi dictatorship was becoming a reality.
---
[slow, dark]
In 1934, Hitler moved to consolidate his control over the state and the military.
During the purge known as the Night of the Long Knives…
The regime murdered political rivals and leaders of the SA, along with other victims.
[short pause]
When President Hindenburg died in August 1934…
Hitler combined the offices of Chancellor and President.
He declared himself Führer — the supreme leader of Germany.
[grave, deliberate]
The armed forces were required to swear an oath of personal loyalty to Hitler.
Germany had become a dictatorship.
---
[slow, reflective]
Hitler's rise was not inevitable.
It was made possible by economic catastrophe…
Political division…
Extremist propaganda…
Antisemitism…
Street violence…
And the failure of democratic leaders to defend the republic.
[short pause]
But one decision was especially decisive.
[dramatic, lower tone]
On the thirtieth of January, 1933…
Conservative elites helped place Hitler in the highest political office in Germany.
They believed they could control him.
[long pause]
Instead…
They helped destroy the democracy they claimed to protect.
---
[somber, emotional]
Within a few years, Hitler's dictatorship would lead Germany and Europe into a devastating war.
The Nazi regime would carry out the Holocaust…
The systematic murder of six million Jews…
Alongside the persecution and murder of millions of other victims.
[slow, grave]
The lesson of this history is not simply that one man came to power.
It is that democracy can be weakened step by step…
Until the people who believe they are safe discover that the institutions meant to protect them are gone.
[dramatic, slow]
Germany, 1933.
A democracy destroyed.
A dictatorship established.
And a world moving toward catastrophe.
[long pause]
The rise of Hitler…
And the beginning of one of the darkest chapters in human history.
