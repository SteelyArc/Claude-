[00:06] By 1795 it had vanished from the map entirely. How does a giant simply disappear?

[00:29] To the east lay Lithuania – vast, powerful, and the last pagan state in Europe.
[00:36] The Union of Krewo arranged a marriage. Jogaila was baptised, married Jadwiga and became King Władysław II Jagiełło.
[00:44] Together they crushed their common enemy, the Teutonic Knights, at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410.
[00:55] 1569. The last Jagiellonian king, Sigismund II Augustus, had no son. The union risked falling apart…
[01:03] …so the Union of Lublin merged Poland and Lithuania into one Commonwealth: one parliament, one elected king.
[01:11] Its lands now belong to Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Belarus, Ukraine and parts of Russia and Estonia.
[01:19] Catholics, Orthodox, Protestants, Jews and Muslim Tatars lived side by side. In 1573 the Warsaw Confederation guaranteed religious peace.
[01:31] With no dynasty left, kings were now elected – by any nobleman who turned up. Tens of thousands voted in a field near Warsaw.
[01:41] Every new king had to swear to the Henrician Articles, handing much of his power to the nobles.
[01:50] The nobility – the szlachta – ran the country. Most peasants were serfs, bound to their lords’ land.
[01:58] Laws needed unanimous consent. From 1652, a single deputy could cancel an entire session of parliament: the liberum veto.
[02:07] Over the next century, dozens of parliaments ended without passing a single law. Government slowly ground to a halt.
[02:19] Yet on the battlefield it was feared. At Kircholm in 1605, its winged hussars beat a Swedish army about three times their size.
[02:28] In 1610 its army even marched into Moscow and occupied the Kremlin.
[02:35] By 1619 the Commonwealth covered almost one million km² – its greatest extent.
[02:45] 1648. The Cossacks of Ukraine rose up under Bohdan Khmelnytsky against Polish lords and Catholic rule.
[02:53] In 1654 the Cossacks swore loyalty to the Russian Tsar at Pereiaslav. Russia invaded within months.
[03:01] Russian armies poured across the border, taking Smolensk, Vilnius and much of the east.
[03:08] Then in 1655 Sweden invaded too. Warsaw and Kraków fell. Poles call it “the Deluge”.
[03:15] War, famine and plague killed between a quarter and a third of the population.
[03:22] The Truce of Andrusovo (1667) handed Smolensk, Kyiv and eastern Ukraine to Russia – for good.
[03:33] 1683. A huge Ottoman army besieged Vienna, the Habsburg capital.
[03:39] Poland’s King Jan III Sobieski rode to the rescue.
[03:45] His charge of some 18,000 horsemen – among the largest in history – broke the siege. It was the Commonwealth’s last great triumph.
[03:57] 1697. The Elector of Saxony bought enough votes to be elected King Augustus II. Foreign money now chose Poland’s kings.
[04:06] In the Great Northern War (1700–1721), Swedish and Russian armies marched across Poland as if it were their own.
[04:14] Peter the Great’s victory at Poltava in 1709 made Russia the master of Eastern Europe.
[04:22] In 1717 the “Silent Sejm”, surrounded by Russian troops, capped the army at 24,000 men. Russia now “guaranteed” Poland’s freedoms.
[04:30] Its neighbours kept armies many times larger.
[04:42] 1764. Russia’s Catherine the Great put her former lover, Stanisław August Poniatowski, on the Polish throne.
[04:49] 1772: the First Partition. Russia, Prussia and Austria seized nearly 30% of its land and over a third of its people.
[04:59] Europe’s cartoonists drew the monarchs carving up “the Polish cake”.
[05:05] Shocked into action, Poland passed Europe’s first modern written constitution on 3 May 1791.
[05:14] Catherine would not allow it. In 1792 Russian troops invaded, and the constitution was crushed.
[05:21] 1793: the Second Partition. Russia and Prussia took another huge share. What was left could barely survive.
[05:29] 1794. Tadeusz Kościuszko, a veteran of the American Revolution, led a national uprising. Peasants armed with scythes won at Racławice.
[05:39] But in October he was wounded and captured at Maciejowice, and Russian troops stormed Warsaw.
[05:46] 1795: the Third Partition. The Commonwealth was wiped off the map.
[05:54] For the next 123 years, there would be no Poland on the map of Europe.
[06:29] Poland finally returned to the map in 1918 – but the old Commonwealth was gone forever.
